Opening a grocery store: the checklist to start well

Opening a grocery store takes little equipment but a lot of organisation. This checklist helps you start with the right stock, fair prices and simple tracking from day one.

In this guide
  1. Choose the location and know your customers
  2. Set your starting budget
  3. Build the first stock
  4. Set prices and margin
  5. Organise tracking from day one

Choose the location and know your customers

Watch foot traffic, neighbouring shops and what people buy most. A grocery store succeeds first through proximity: everyday products (rice, oil, sugar, drinks, soap) must always be available.

Set your starting budget

  • First stock of goods.
  • Rent or deposit, and local licence or permits.
  • Shelves, scales, a fridge if needed.
  • A cash reserve for the first months.
  • A phone or computer to manage sales.

Build the first stock

Start with products that sell every day, in small quantities, rather than a big stock of slow ones. Compare at least two suppliers, negotiate bulk prices and write down every purchase price.

Set prices and margin

Calculate your margin on each product (see our margin guide) and compare with neighbouring shops. Keep a thinner margin on heavily compared products (rice, sugar) and a bigger one on convenience items.

Organise tracking from day one

Frequently asked questions

Opening a grocery store: the checklist to start well

How much does it take to open a small grocery store?

It depends on the country and size; mostly plan for the first stock, rent and a cash reserve for the first months.

Should you give customers credit from the start?

Better to begin carefully: credit limited to known customers, with a clear ceiling and deadline, written in a book.
