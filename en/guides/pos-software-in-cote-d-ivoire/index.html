What a till must do for a business in Côte d’Ivoire

Whether you run maquis and restaurants, grocery shops and boutiques, bakeries and hardware stores and depots, the till must check out fast, give the right change, keep a record of every sale with its payment method and update the stock.

The CFA franc has no cents: set prices in round amounts (for example multiples of 25 or 50 FCFA) so giving change is easy.

Payments to support in Côte d’Ivoire

Your customers can pay by cash, Wave, Orange Money, MTN MoMo and card. Good POS software lets you record the method of each sale and shows the total of each in the evening.

  • Cash: the change due calculates itself; count the float in the morning and check the discrepancy in the evening.
  • Wave: the customer pays from their phone; wait for the confirmation before confirming the sale, then reconcile the total every evening.
  • Orange Money: the customer pays from their phone; wait for the confirmation before confirming the sale, then reconcile the total every evening.
  • MTN MoMo: the customer pays from their phone; wait for the confirmation before confirming the sale, then reconcile the total every evening.
  • Card: record it as a payment method so you find it in your totals; the payment terminal stays your bank’s.

Working without internet

A till that stops when the network drops loses sales. Choose software that keeps taking payments offline and sends the sales as soon as the network returns: Kaislo does.

Keep the phone charged and, if possible, a power bank: the till depends on the device.

Five checks before choosing in Côte d’Ivoire

  • Is the price shown in FCFA, with no exchange-rate surprise?
  • Can all your customers’ payment methods (cash, Wave, Orange Money, MTN MoMo and card) be recorded?
  • Does the software work without internet?
  • Can you export your data (Excel, PDF) and take it with you if you leave?
  • What requirements exist in Côte d’Ivoire for cash register hardware or software? Ask the tax authority or a local accountant.
