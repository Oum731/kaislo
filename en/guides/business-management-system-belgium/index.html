A management system for a shop in Belgium: stock, sales and customer credit

A shop management system brings sales, stock, customer credit and the day’s accounts together in one place. For a Belgian neighbourhood shop, what matters is that it is simple, works without internet and keeps your euro figures ready for your accountant.

What a management system must do

  • Record each sale with its payment method (cash, card, Payconiq).
  • Update stock with every sale and warn you before stock-outs.
  • Track customer credit: who owes how much, since when.
  • Give you each evening the turnover, cash gaps and best-selling products.
  • Export data (Excel, PDF) for your accountant.

Choose according to your business

A grocery store or shop needs an item catalogue, prices and reliable stock. A business with several sellers needs separate permissions (who can give a discount or cancel a sale). A business that sells on credit needs a clear credit book.

If in doubt, choose the simplest system your sellers will really use: a complete tool nobody uses is worth nothing.

Start in one week

  • Day 1: create the business and enter your twenty best sellers with their prices.
  • Days 2 and 3: take payments with the system alongside your usual method and compare totals.
  • Day 4: count the stock and correct the gaps.
  • Days 5 to 7: add the rest of your items and train your sellers.
  • End of week: read the evening accounts and decide to continue or adjust.

Common mistakes at the start

  • Entering the whole catalogue at once instead of starting with the twenty best sellers.
  • Skipping the opening stock count: displayed stock is wrong from day one.
  • Forgetting to record credit sales, then losing track of who owes what.
  • Letting several people take payments on the same account: you cannot tell who sold what.
  • Never comparing the software total with the cash drawer at the end of the day.

The numbers to follow every week

  • This week’s sales, compared with last week.
  • Margin: selling price minus purchase price, on your ten best sellers.
  • Items out of stock or close to it.
  • Unpaid customer credit and how old it is.
  • Items that no longer sell and tie up cash.

Euro prices and payments

Amounts and discounts are handled in euros with cents. You record the payment method of each sale; the card terminal stays your bank’s and Kaislo never collects the money. For VAT, keep consistent prices and keep your totals for your accountant: Kaislo does not replace your VAT return.

Frequently asked questions

A management system for a shop in Belgium: stock, sales and customer credit

Which numbers should a shop follow every week?

Sales, margin on the best sellers, stock-outs, customer credit to collect and slow-moving items. Five numbers are enough to run a small shop.

Does a management system replace the accountant?

No. It organises your sales and stock and makes exporting for your accountant easier, but bookkeeping and tax obligations remain the accountant’s responsibility.

Which management system for a small shop in Belgium?

A simple tool that covers sales, stock and customer credit, works without internet and lets you export data. Test it for a few days with your real items.

Do I need a stock management system?

As soon as you have more than a few dozen items, yes: it prevents stock-outs, hidden losses and recounting by hand.

Does Kaislo handle VAT?

Kaislo records your sales and prices. It does not replace your VAT return: keep your totals and check with your accountant.
